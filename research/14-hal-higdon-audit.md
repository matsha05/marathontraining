## Where Hal Higdon Excels (We Should Learn From)

### 1. Simple, Named Tiers
Higdon has 6 distinct plans: Novice 1, Novice 2, Novice Supreme, Intermediate 1, Intermediate 2, Advanced 1, Advanced 2.

**Each has a clear identity:**
- "Novice 1 = first marathon, 4 days/week, 20-mile peak"
- "Intermediate 1 = back-to-back Saturday/Sunday runs"
- "Advanced 1 = speedwork + 3 x 20-milers"

**What we lack:** Our plan generator is adaptive but doesn't give users a "you're on the Intermediate plan" identity. Users want to say "I'm doing Higdon Intermediate 1" or "I'm on Hansons Advanced."

**Fix:** Add named plan tiers (e.g., "Foundation," "Hybrid," "Performance") so users have an identity.

### 2. Crystal-Clear Weekly Structure
Higdon's Novice 1:
- Mon: Rest
- Tue: 3-mile run
- Wed: 5-mile run
- Thu: 3-mile run
- Fri: Rest
- Sat: Cross-train
- Sun: Long run (progressive)

It's dead simple. Users can glance at the week and know exactly what to do.

**What we lack:** Our plans are more complex (strength + running + durability). Complexity is a feature for hybrid athletes but could overwhelm novices.

**Fix:** Ensure our "simple view" is as clean as Higdon's grid. Consider a "novice mode" that hides complexity.

### 3. The 20-Mile Cap
Higdon caps long runs at 20 miles across all levels. His rationale: running longer in training increases injury risk more than it helps.

**Our approach:** We use a time-based cap (2:15–3:00 depending on level), which is actually better practice for mixed-pace runners. But we don't communicate "20 miles is enough" as clearly.

### 4. Stepback Weeks Are Predictable
Higdon: Every 3rd week is a stepback (reduced mileage).

**Our approach:** We use 4-week blocks (3 build + 1 deload), which aligns with Daniels. Slightly different rhythm, both valid.

## Where We're Ahead of Higdon

### 1. VDOT-Based Pacing
Higdon uses "conversational pace" and "30-90 seconds slower than marathon pace" as guidance. It's vague.

**We use:** Daniels' VDOT system with precise E/M/T/I/R zones calculated from race performance.

**This is objectively better** for runners who want accuracy.

### 2. Strength Training Integration
Higdon mentions cross-training (swimming, cycling) but has **zero strength guidance.**

**We have:** Full strength integration with interference-aware scheduling, volume modulation across phases, and lift selection for runners.

**This is our differentiator** for hybrid athletes.

### 3. Missed Workout Logic
Higdon's advice: "Listen to your body" or "drop the workout."

**We have:** A full decision tree for missed workouts (illness vs life, priority-based rescheduling, Pfitz thresholds for goal revision).

### 4. Injury/Pain Logic
Higdon: "See a doctor if it hurts."

**We have:** Injury history integration, pain severity routing, automatic plan adjustments.

### 5. Durability/Mobility
Higdon: Minimal mention of mobility work.

**We have:** Dicharry and Starrett-based durability circuits, pre-run prep, and movement screens.

## Honest Assessment

**Hal Higdon's strength:** Accessibility, trust, and simplicity. Millions of runners have finished their first marathon using his plans. He's proven.

**The Long Game's strength:** Scientific precision, strength integration, and adaptive logic. We serve a niche Higdon doesn't: hybrid athletes who want to keep lifting.

**Are we credible?** Yes — our methodology is anchored to Daniels, Hansons, Seiler, and Pfitzinger, which are more rigorous than Higdon's approach.

**Are we as helpful as Higdon?** Not yet for pure novices. His simplicity is a feature. We need a "novice mode" that matches his clarity while preserving our depth for advanced users.

---

## Recommended Actions

1. **Add plan tier names** — "Foundation," "Hybrid," "Performance"
2. **Add Higdon to Coach Brain** — He's earned a spot as reference for accessibility
3. **Create "simple view"** — Hide strength/durability for users who just want running
4. **Add 3/1 long run option** — Advanced long run pattern
5. **Consider back-to-back pattern** — Intermediate tired-legs option
