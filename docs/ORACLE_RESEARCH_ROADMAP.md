# Oracle Research Roadmap

> Research topics needed to make The Long Game world-class

---

## Priority 1: Ultra Training Methodology

**Goal:** Bake ultra support (50K → 100M) into the plan generator from day one.

**Key Questions:**
- How does periodization differ from marathon? (Time on feet vs. pace specificity)
- Back-to-back long runs: frequency, ratio, timing in training cycle?
- Vertical gain integration for trail ultras
- Fueling/hydration as a trainable skill (not just race-day logistics)
- Ultra-specific strength work (hiking, poles, downhill eccentric prep)
- Taper differences by distance (50K vs. 100M)
- Cutback week patterns for high-volume blocks

**Sources to Query:**
- Jason Koop: *Training Essentials for Ultrarunning*
- Steve House & Scott Johnston: *Training for the Uphill Athlete*
- Kilian Jornet training philosophy (if documented)
- Maffetone Method (MAF) for aerobic base
- Zach Bitter, Courtney Dauwalter training approaches
- Research on ultra pacing strategies (positive splits are common/optimal?)

---

## Priority 2: Elite CrossFit Gym WOD Programming

**Goal:** Build a WOD engine that outputs workouts like the best CrossFit gyms — with RX, Scaled, and Beginner versions.

**Key Questions:**
- How do elite gyms (CompTrain, Misfit, HWPO, Mayhem) structure their strength days?
- What's the pattern for RX → Scaled → Beginner progressions?
- How do they balance monostructural (row/bike/ski) vs. mixed-modal?
- Time domains: What percentage of WODs are in each bucket (5-10 min, 10-20 min, 20-40 min)?
- How do they program for athletes who also run?

**Sources to Query:**
- CompTrain methodology (Ben Bergeron)
- HWPO (Mat Fraser's programming)
- Mayhem (Rich Froning)
- CrossFit Linchpin (Pat Sherwood)
- Marcus Filly's Functional Bodybuilding (hybrid-friendly)
- Research on CrossFit + endurance interference

---

## Priority 3: Movement Scaling Progressions

**Goal:** For every movement in the WOD library, define clear RX → Scaled → Beginner progressions.

**Key Questions:**
- What's the standard progression for each major movement class?
  - Pull: Pull-up → Banded → Ring row → Inverted row
  - Push: HSPU → Box HSPU → Pike push-up → DB press
  - Squat: Pistol → Assisted pistol → Air squat
  - Hinge: Snatch → Hang snatch → DB snatch → KB swing
- Load scaling: What percentage drops are typical (RX 135# → Scaled 95# → Beginner 65#)?
- Rep scaling: Reduce reps or use different movement?
- Time cap vs. round cap approaches

**Sources to Query:**
- CrossFit Level 2 scaling guide
- CompTrain scaling philosophy
- Adaptive/inclusive CrossFit resources
- CrossFit Games scaling history

---

## Priority 4: Runner-Specific Movement Interference

**Goal:** Quantify how specific movements affect running performance so the WOD engine can make smart choices.

**Key Questions:**
- DOMS timelines by movement type (squats vs. lunges vs. RDL)
- Eccentric load rankings (which movements create most soreness?)
- CNS fatigue from heavy lifts vs. high-rep metcons
- Recovery timeline data: How many hours until running feels normal?
- Can we create a "leg fatigue score" for each movement?

**Sources to Query:**
- Concurrent training research (Wilson, Nader, Hickson studies)
- Time-course of DOMS studies
- Alex Viada's hybrid athlete guidelines
- Strength training for runners meta-analyses

---

## Priority 5: VDOT Calibration for Beginners

**Goal:** Make VDOT more accurate for athletes developing running economy.

**Key Questions:**
- How much does running economy improve in year 1 vs. year 3?
- Should we use "adjusted VDOT" for strength athletes new to running?
- What input methods are most accurate? (Race time, time trial, perceived effort)
- Should we auto-calibrate VDOT based on completed workouts + HR data?

**Sources to Query:**
- Daniels' own guidance on VDOT for beginners
- Running economy studies (Saunders, Bassett)
- Garmin/Strava VO2max estimation methodology
- Heart rate drift as a proxy for fitness
